Rendering 101

How to actually get a finished file out of whatever you built your animation in. Works no matter what you used to build it, or what you're editing in.

This is the part people skip. You build something great, then export it wrong, and it looks worse than what was on your screen. Here's the fix.

Rule #1: your build tool is not your delivery tool

After Effects, Blender, whatever you used to build your animation, that's where you build. It's not built to be where you deliver from.

Build Tool
→
Final File

Don't render your final file straight out of your build tool. Ever.

Two stages

Build Tool
→
Intermediate File
→
Video Editor
→
Final File
Why two stages? If you export your final format directly, you're locked in. No colour pass, no fixing a bad frame, no re-export in a different format if someone asks.

Stage 1: render your intermediate

In your build tool's render queue, export ProRes. Two flavours, and the choice comes down to one question: does this need to keep transparency?

No alpha
ProRes 422
Use this for anything with a solid background.
Alpha
ProRes 4444
Use this if you need the transparency channel carried through.

This file will be big. That's fine, it's not the final, it's a stepping stone.

Stage 2: finish in a real video editor

Bring your intermediate file into a proper video editor. Do your colour grade, your edit, whatever pass it needs.

Export your actual final file from there, not from your build tool. For most projects, that's H.264 in an MP4 container.

Frame rate and compression

Frame rate
Depends on the project: 24, 30, or 60fps, no single standard. Match whatever your comp was built at, and don't change it at export.
Compression
No fixed rule either. Depends on the project and what it's for. Use your judgement, or ask if you're not sure.

Build a template

💡
Set up your render queue settings and your editor's export settings once, then save them as a template. Every project after that is a couple of clicks instead of rebuilding it from scratch.

The whole rule

Same rule applies no matter what you built with:

  1. 1
    Render an intermediate
    Out of your build tool, usually an image sequence, for flexibility.
  2. 2
    Composite and finish
    Bring it into your editor, if needed.
  3. 3
    Export your final file
    From the editor, not the build tool.
Build ≠ Deliver

That's the whole thing, and it doesn't change no matter what software you're using.
