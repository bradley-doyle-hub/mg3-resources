A suggested plan for the break

You don't have to follow this. It keeps the render and export from piling up in the last two days.

  1. Before the break: Model chosen, materials started, lighting started.
  2. During the break: Finish the materials and lighting. Animate your hero move. Test-render a few single frames.
  3. Early in Week 09: Start the full render. Cycles can take a long time, so don't wait until the last night.
  4. Before Week 09 class: Composite and export in After Effects, so you can ask questions in class.
  5. Friday, November 6th by 11:59 PM: Submit your 1920x1080 mp4.

Before you download

  • A .blend file opens straight in Blender. For .fbx, .obj or .glb files, use File, Import.
  • Check the licence, and keep a note of where each model came from.
  • Look at the preview from several angles. Models that look good in the thumbnail can be messy up close.
  • AI-generated models often have messy geometry and unusable textures. If it looks odd up close, pick a different one.
  • Imported models can come in the wrong size or facing the wrong way. Check scale and rotation before you build anything on top.

Look at real photos of your product before you build the material. Notice where the highlights sit and how soft the edges of the shadows are.

Quick lighting ideas for a product

  • Start with three lights: a main (key) light, a softer fill, and a rim light behind the product to separate it from the background.
  • A big soft light gives soft, flattering reflections. A small hard light gives sharp highlights and hard shadows.
  • Reflective and metallic products need something to reflect. An HDRI or a few large softboxes will do more than lots of small lights.
  • Keep the background simple so the product reads first.

Check before you render the full sequence

  • Resolution is 1920x1080 and the frame range is correct.
  • Output is a PNG image sequence, saved to its own folder.
  • Frame rate matches your After Effects composition.
  • Denoising is on if you're using Cycles.
  • Render a single frame at full size first, and look at it closely.
  • To render the whole animation, use Render, Render Animation (Ctrl+F12).

Troubleshooting

ProblemTry this
The render is blackCheck there's a light (or an HDRI with strength above 0). Check you're looking through the camera (Numpad 0) and the camera isn't inside your object.
The render is grainy or noisyTurn on Denoise (Cycles, Render properties, Sampling). Raise the samples a little.
Tiny bright speckles (fireflies)Lower the strength of very bright lights. Try Clamping in Light Paths. Denoising also helps.
Textures show up pinkBlender can't find the image. Use File, External Data, Find Missing Files.
The imported object is the wrong size or sidewaysCheck its scale and rotation. Apply them (Ctrl+A) before animating.
The render takes foreverTest in Eevee or at a lower resolution percentage. Lower the samples while testing. Turn on GPU rendering in Preferences, System.
Blender crashedFile, Recover, Auto Save (or Last Session). Save versions often, using the Week 04 naming system.

Add-ons

Some add-ons come with Blender and just need to be turned on in Preferences. Newer versions of Blender also have an Extensions section for finding more. You don't need any add-ons for this project.

  • Node Wrangler: speeds up working with materials. Select a Principled BSDF node and press Ctrl+Shift+T to load a whole set of texture maps at once.
  • BlenderKit: free models and materials from inside Blender. blenderkit.com
  • Images as Planes: used in the Blender Secrets decals video above.
