YouTube Tutorial Channels

A short list of channels for more practice outside class. Blender first, then After Effects, each with a clear place to start.

💡
Not sure where to begin? Blender: the Donut series from Blender Guru. After Effects: SonduckFilm. Come back to the rest of the list once those click.

Blender

Roughly in order, from first project to going deeper.

Blender Guru (Andrew Price) — start here. The Donut series covers modeling, texturing, lighting, and rendering in one project.
Grant Abbitt — calmer pace than Blender Guru, good second stop for anyone who found the Donut series fast.
Polyfjord — animation and motion design focused, high production value, good once you're past the basics.
Ryan King Art — procedural materials and shaders, for going deeper on texturing.
Blender Secrets — one-to-two-minute videos on single tools, good for "how do I do X" questions rather than full tutorials.
Blender (official channel) — release notes and new features. Not a tutorial channel, but worth knowing it exists.

After Effects

Same idea: start at the top, work down as you need more.

SonduckFilm — start here. Huge library (1,300+ videos), beginner-friendly, many with downloadable project files.
Ben Marriott — approachable tutorials for beginner to intermediate, plus general career and workflow tips.
After Effects Basics — weekly, concise lessons on keyframing, masking, and motion fundamentals.
Jake In Motion — motion design workflows, expressions, and software updates.
After Effects Tutorials w/ Mikey — practical instruction from beginner through advanced.
Adobe Creative Cloud (official) — feature updates and showcases. Not a tutorial channel, but worth knowing it exists.
✓
That's the list. Pick one channel, follow one project through to the end, then branch out.
